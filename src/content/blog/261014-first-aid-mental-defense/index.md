As of now, it has been a couple months since I started out at this job.

The office is quite a stressful place, where people pile deadlines on each other always, every hour of the working day.
Fortunately, the mutual commiseration means that there is really little strife or conflict. But I guess blowing up emotionally would get you fired on the spot, so this is kind of a forced situation.

The one observation I want to talk about is how people react to comments from others, or off-hand remarks. Of course, they are influenced by the overall culture, but this is about dealing with comments that one might not take well.
It's generally not good to lash out, ever; especially when one cannot infer the true intention behind remarks. What stood out to me is that, each person has their own style of communication, developed to cope with the environment.

A senior's remark might be snide, but it might be because it's how they learned to deal with their previous peers, and not actually with bad intentions.
One senior always responds with a quick quip. Another always jokes with a hint of lowering themselves. It's how they respond to offhand remarks from higher-ups, to defuse the pressure; now that I'm here they use the same style of conversation in our chats, almost like an instinctual response.

Now how does this develop? I seem to have picked up a number of these tricks myself - quick jokes in a lassez-faire tone for defusal, creative appeasement to ward off messages pushing deadlines, rerouting requests to avoid piling work to myself, et cetera.

Once I see it, I see it everywhere. When people socialize, they always have their own style of dealing with an encounter. Aggressive, enthusiastic; cordial, restrained. The public personality is one that is shaped by the enviroment (and somewtimes, painful experiences) to deal with the public matters.

It's somewhat of a survival skill, I would say, one that is in the realm of emotional intelligence. One that is gained after getting over stubbornness, and childish rightousness. One that is essential when you are at those above's beck and call, and their approval is what keeps you around. One that is even more essential, when you are on your own in society.

I certainly felt like I have changed. Is this what they call maturity?
